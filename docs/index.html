About the five atomic models

Static, crawlable reference — how our picture of the atom evolved from a solid sphere to probability clouds.

1803

Dalton — Solid Sphere

Each element is made of tiny, indivisible, identical spheres. Mass distinguishes elements; they combine in fixed whole-number ratios to form compounds.

Limitation: no internal structure
1904

Thomson — Plum Pudding

A diffuse positively charged sphere with electrons embedded throughout, like plums in a pudding. The atom is neutral overall.

Limitation: no nucleus, no spectra
1911

Rutherford — Nuclear

Atoms are mostly empty space with a tiny, dense, positive nucleus. Electrons orbit at a distance, like planets.

Limitation: classical orbits collapse
1913

Bohr — Quantised Orbits

Electrons occupy fixed circular orbits with quantised energies, grouped into shells (n = 1, 2, 3…). Works exactly for hydrogen.

Limitation: fails for many-electron atoms
1926

Quantum — Probability Clouds

Electrons are described by wave functions whose square gives probability density — orbitals (s, p, d, f), not fixed paths. The modern picture.

Limitation: many-electron atoms need approximations
