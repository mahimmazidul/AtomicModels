Rutherford — Nuclear (1911)

Principle

Ernest Rutherford concluded from alpha-particle scattering that an atom is mostly empty space with a tiny, massive, positively charged nucleus at its centre; electrons orbit at a great distance, much like planets around the Sun.

Significance

The nuclear model introduced the nucleus and the modern concept of empty atomic volume. It explained scattering data and located essentially all of an atom’s mass in its centre.

Limitations

A classical orbiting electron should continuously radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus in a fraction of a second, so the model is dynamically unstable. It also cannot explain discrete atomic spectra.
